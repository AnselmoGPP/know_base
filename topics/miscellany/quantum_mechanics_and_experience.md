## Superposition

Let's consider the following:

- A particle (like an electron) and two particular physical properties it can have (color and hardness). 
- Only 2 possible colors (black, white) and hardnesses (soft, hard) have ever been seen.
- A "Color box" can measure the color of an electron. It has 3 apertures (in, b, w). Electrons are fed trough in. Black electrons leave through b. White electrons leave through w.
- A "Hardness box" works similary. Apertures: in, s, h.
- There are no correlations between color and hardness.
- We don't tamper the electrons between boxes, unless it's explicitly declared.

__Experiment 1__: An electron passing through a series of color boxes, with no tampering between boxes, always gets out through the same apperture (black or white). Something similar happens with hardness boxes. Box measurements are repeatable.

__Experiment 2__: Let's put a color box, a hardness box, and a color box one after another. An electron that leaves the 1st box through w (white), and the 2nd box through s (soft), will leave the 3rd box through w (50% of times) or b (the other 50%). The same happens for any result in the first 2 boxes, and when we replace the boxes with the other type.

- Apparently, the hardness box applies color tampering. It doesn't modify hardness, but disrupts color.
- What determines which electrons change color and which don't? Fact: both type of electrons (those who change color and those who doesn't) need not initially differ from one another in any way whatever.
- Can we build a better hardness box that doesn't disrupts color? Fact: it doesn't matter how careful or which method we use to build a box, the 50-50 statistics doesn't change a bit.

Consider a color-and-hardness box that measures both color and hardness. Thus, it has 4 exits (ws, wh, bs, bh). This box could consist of a hardness box and a color box ("sub-boxes"). However, the last sub-box could alter the result of the first sub-box. It seems that we cannot determine both states at the same time.

**Uncertainty principle**: Measurable properties are incompatible with one another when measurements of one will (so far as we know) always necessarily disrupt the other.
