## Introduction

The **Known Space** is small region in the Milky Way galaxy centered on Earth. The **Earth** is ruled by the **United Nations**. Humans have explored the Solar System and colonized dozens of nearby systems within the Known Space. They even made contact with other species, such as:

- **Outsiders**: Many-limbed. They're estimated to be the most advanced species in Known Space. They created the Faster-Than-Light (FTL) propulsion, or **hyperdrive**.
- **Pierson's Puppeteers**: Two-headed herbivores. Extremely intelligent but pathologically cautious.
- **Kzinti**: Aggressive felinoids.
- **Paks**: Humanoid species from planet **Pakhome**, near the Galactic Core. They have 3 life stages: Child (cared for by Breeders), Breeder (mature), Protector (extreme intelligence, strength, and protection instinct). The Protector stage is triggered by eating the Tree-of-life root, which contains a virus. Protectors are hyper-paranoid and usually consider other species as existential threats. Humans descend from a lost colony of Pak Breeders stranded on Earth 2.5 million years ago. The Tree-of-life couldn't grow in Earth's soil, so Protectors died out and Breeders evolved into _Homo sapiens_.

## Fleet of Worlds

(~200 years before Ringworld)

The **Fleet of Worlds** is a mobile planetary system of the **Pierson's Puppeteers**. The planets are their homeworld Hearth and four agricultural "Natural Preserves" (NP1, NP2, NP3, NP4). They fled Known Space, and the galaxy, after discovering a wave of supernovae explosions at the galactic core that would sterilize the galaxy, taking their worlds with them via advanced drive systems (reactionless drives purchased from the Outsiders) away from the Galaxy. Since they generate so much waste heat from its massive population and industries, they don't need stellar heating.

The Puppeteers secretly keep a population of humans in NP4. Raised as a subservient farming class, they believe they owe their survival to Puppeteer benevolence, who rescued them from a derelict colony ship. They don't even know about Earth.

**Kirsten Quinn-Kovacs** is a brilliant human engineer from NP4. She and her crew are selected for a deep-space scouting mission with the starship **Explorer** under the supervision of the puppeteer **Nessus**. Their mission: scout ahead of the fleet for potential dangers.

Through exploration and investigation, the humans discover their real origins: About 400 years ago, a **Pak Protector** (Phssthpok) was traveling towards Earth in search of lost Pak descendants when he encountered the **Long Pass**, a ship carrying human colonists fleeing a dying Earth. He killed the adult crew, intending to use the human embryos aboard to reestablish a Pak colony. However, the Puppeteers intercepted the ship (year 2198), drove off the Pak, and seized the embryos and a surviving infant. These humans (**Colonists**) were used to create a population of humans in NP4. They were lied about their origins to ensure loyalty and docility.

Revealing the truth sparks a quiet rebellion. The humans realize they are not guests but slaves, used to maintain the fleet’s agricultural systems. Humans start gaining autonomy, ending up asserting their independence and becoming potential partners of the Puppeteers instead of slaves.

## Juggler of Worlds

**Sigmund Ausfaller** is an agent of Earth’s ARM (Amalgamated Regional Militia) that investigates a series of anomalies tied to the sudden disappearance of the Puppeteers from Known Space. Key events include:

- A scientific ship is found with its General Products hull intact, but the crew dead from extreme tidal forces, which evidence it drifted too close to a neutron star.
- The Puppeteers abruptly sell off or abandon their assets, including their revolutionary technology, triggering economic collapse.
- Pirates begin pulling ships out of hyperspace, a feat only possible with Puppeteer tech.
- The world’s richest man, Nobel Prize winner Carlos Wu, appears involved in suspicious dealings.

Sigmund uncovers that the Puppeteers, having learned the galactic core is exploding, have fled the galaxy with their Fleet of Worlds. Their departure destabilizes human society, and their covert agents, like the Puppeteer scout **Nessus**, continue manipulating events from the shadows.

The Puppeteers, led by the **Hindmost**, orchestrated their escape while covering their tracks. The Hindmost balanced multiple crises (internal dissent, external threats, and the logistics of moving entire planets) while maintaining secrecy:

- They leak advanced technology to key individuals to create distractions and dependencies.
- They manipulate human politics, including inciting birth-license riots to weaken Earth’s stability.
- They use agents like Nessus to infiltrate human institutions and monitor threats.
- They prepare the Fleet’s planets for interstellar travel using reactionless drives.

Sigmund is captured and interrogated by Puppeteers. Though he pieces together much of the truth, he dies before he can expose the full scope of their plan. His investigations, however, lay the groundwork for future human understanding of the Puppeteer exodus.

## Destroyer of Worlds

(10 years later)

The **Pak Protector** Thssthfok, who is recalled from a planetary survey mission when news spreads that **Pakhome** (Pak homeworld) is doomed by the expanding wave of supernovae from the galactic core. All Protectors are summoned to join a massive exodus (an armada of slower-than-light colony ships) fleeing outward through the galaxy.

Unlike the Puppeteers’ stealthy escape, the Pak take a brutal approach: they destroy any advanced civilization in their path, eliminating potential future threats to their descendants. Entire inhabited worlds are annihilated.

The Earth's ARM agent **Sigmund Ausfaller**, now living in the Fleet of Worlds, receives a distress signal from the **Gw'oth** (sapient, starfish-like beings), a newly discovered aquatic species on the icy moon **Jm'ho**. The Gw'oth can form group minds (up to 16 individuals merging into one consciousness) and have detected the approaching Pak fleet.

Sigmund, accompanied by humans **Eric** and **Kirsten van Riebeek**, Puppeteer **Baedeker**, and Gw'oth representative **Er'o**, investigates the threat. They confirm the Pak are destroying planets ahead of the Fleet of Worlds’ trajectory, putting both **New Terra** (formerly NP4) and the Puppeteers in mortal danger.

The Puppeteers prefer flight or concealment over confrontation, but their planets are too slow to outrun the Pak. Hiding becomes the strategy, masking their presence and hoping the Pak armada passes by unnoticed. Meanwhile, the liberated humans of New Terra are willing to fight, but lack the military strength.

The team captures a Pak ship and takes a Pak Protector prisoner. The Protector proves extremely dangerous (intelligent, resourceful, and driven by an unshakable imperative to protect his bloodline). The encounter reveals the deep biological and cultural divide between humans and their Pak ancestors.

The group devises a plan to misdirect the Pak armada by fabricating evidence of a false destination. They use the captive Pak Protector to send a false signal suggesting a large human population exists far off the Fleet of Worlds’ trajectory. The Gw'oth, with their unique group intelligence, help refine the deception, ensuring it appears credible. Since Pak Protectors are biologically driven to secure safe worlds for their descendants, the armada’s leaders are convinced that a better, inhabited target lies elsewhere. The ruse succeeds, and the Pak fleet alters course, bypassing the Fleet of Worlds.

## Betrayer of Worlds

(70 years before Ringworld)

**Louis Wu** (human) is stranded on **Wunderland** during a brutal civil war between the aristocratic Aristos and the Resistance. He becomes entangled in the conflict while seeking a way to return home. His actions draw the attention of powerful forces, and he eventually discovers a potential escape route, which brings him into contact with both the Gw'oth and Puppeteer machinations.

**Ol't'ro**, a group mind composed of 16 **Gw'oth**, leads a colony that fled tyranny to settle on a distant world. They seek peace and independence, but their new home lies in the projected path of the Fleet of Worlds. When they detect the approaching planets, they realize their survival is at stake and prepare to defend their world.

**Achilles**, a charismatic but treacherous Puppeteer politic, exploits fear and political instability to seize control within the Puppeteer Concordance. Once disgraced, he manipulates events to gain power, driven by a desire for revenge against those who opposed him. This involves staging political coups, murdering rivals, and destabilizing the government. His ambition puts the entire Fleet at risk, as he prioritizes personal vendettas over collective survival. He manipulates the government to redirect the Fleet, risking everything for control.

**Nessus**, the Puppeteer scout, is running out of resources and allies. Once instrumental in early human-Puppeteer interactions, he now operates on the fringes, trying to prevent catastrophe with dwindling influence. He has a deeper understanding of the looming threats.

The **Hindmost** discovers anomalous events suggesting Protectors' presence, rogue stolen Puppeteer ships, and espionage against the Fleet of Worlds. He orders an investigation led by **Nessus**, who recruits the Puppeteer **Traveling Minister** and the human **Bailey**.

A hidden colony of Pak Protectors reveals itself indirectly through attacks and sabotage. They don't care about galactic politics, they are obsessed over survival of their “bloodline”. These Protectors, who have stolen advanced Puppeteer technology, have created a secret base, infiltrated Puppeteer infrastructure, and manipulated some human factions. They consider the Fleet of Worlds a potential threat, and aim to engineer events that will eliminate any threat to their descendants.

Meanwhile, Bailey receives covert instructions from New Terra: Determine whether the Puppeteers are hiding existential information from humanity, and support the survival of New Terra, even against Puppeteer interests. Bailey begins suspecting that the Puppeteers have lied for generations about the status of Earth and broader human space. This suspicion grows when he uncovers inconsistencies in historical records and travel logs.

Nessus discovers that several dangers are converging: A Pak Protector faction has gained access to Puppeteer hyperspace routes, a rogue Puppeteer with idealistic but dangerous beliefs may be collaborating with them, human rebels are plotting an uprising, and a powerful Puppeteer military asset may no longer be under centralized control. The pieces begin to point to a larger conspiracy that could threaten the stability of the Fleet of Worlds and possibly the survival of both Puppeteers and humans.

As Bailey, Nessus, and Traveling Minister investigate, they uncover the secret Pak laboratories using stolen Puppeteer biotech, a massive Protector-built trap intended to manipulate Puppeteer strategic decisions, and evidence that the Pak have reverse-engineered some elements of Puppeteer defense systems. The Protectors aim to push the Puppeteers into fleeing the region prematurely or into a direction favorable to Protector safety, and don't care if this destroys the New Terran humans or Puppeteer political stability.

The Puppeteers have known for centuries that Earth survived the galactic core explosion, contrary to what many humans believed, but they intentionally obscured this fact. Bailey realizes that New Terra’s entire history has been shaped by Puppeteer fear. Many Puppeteers are outraged that such a massive deception was committed by their own leaders.

Several overlapping conflicts take place: Covert battles in space between Puppeteer ships and Protector-controlled vessels, a rescue-and-sabotage mission involving Bailey and human allies, a political coup attempt within the Puppeteer government, and the Protectors’ master plan to redirect Puppeteer migration and secure an uncontested region. Human and Puppeteer operatives work together to prevent the Protectors from forcing a fatal strategic misstep.

The Pak plot against the Fleet of Worlds is dismantled, though not all Protectors are destroyed. Bailey exposes major Puppeteer deceptions, triggering political upheaval. New Terra gains a clearer and more independent political position. The Hindmost loses some power and must recalibrate its plans.

Louis, Ol't'ro, and remnants of the sane Puppeteer leadership worked to avert disaster, exposing Achilles’ schemes. These experiences allowed Louis to improve his skills, cynicism, and deep knowledge of alien politics.

## Ringworld

On Earth in 2850 AD, a bored **Louis Wu** is celebrating his 200th birthday. Despite his age, Louis is in perfect physical condition due to the longevity drug boosterspice. **Nessus**, a Pierson's puppeteer, offers him a mysterious job. Intrigued, Louis accepts. For the rest of the ship's crew, Nessus also recruits the Kzin Speaker-to-Animals (**Speaker**) and **Teela Brown**, a young human woman who becomes Louis's lover.

On Nessus' home world (which is fleeing deadly radiation from a galactic core explosion that will arrive in 20,000 years), they are told that their goal is to determine if the **Ringworld**, a gigantic artificial ring near the puppeteers' path, poses any threat to their migration. The Ringworld is about 1.6 million km wide and approximately the diameter of Earth's orbit, encircling a sunlike star. It rotates to provide artificial gravity 99% as strong as Earth's from centrifugal force. It has a habitable inner surface (equivalent in area to approximately three million Earths), a breathable atmosphere, and a temperature optimal for humans. Night is provided by an inner ring of shadow squares which are connected to each other by thin, ultra-strong wire. When the crew completes their mission, as payment they will be given the starship they used to travel to the puppeteer world, which is about 1000 times faster than any human or Kzinti ship.

When they reach the vicinity of the Ringworld, they are unable to contact anyone. Their ship, the Lying Bastard, is disabled by an automated meteoroid-defense system. The vessel collides with a strand of shadow-square wire and crash-lands near a huge mountain, which is called "Fist-of-God" by the first natives they speak with. The fusion drive is destroyed, so they set out to find a way to get the Lying Bastard off the Ringworld and use the undamaged hyperdrive to return home.

Using their flycycles (personal flying vehicles), they set out for the rim of the ring, searching for technology to help them get home. They encounter primitive human natives who live in the ruins of a once-advanced city. The natives think that Louis is one of the engineers who created the ring, whom they revere as gods. The crew is attacked when Louis accidentally commits what the natives consider a blasphemy, but extricate themselves.

During their journey, Nessus reveals several puppeteer secrets. They initiated research into rendering the Kzinti extinct, considering them dangerous and useless, but found that the numerous Man-Kzin wars (which the Kzinti always lost) had greatly reduced their aggression: a very high percentage of Kzinti males were killed in each conflict, leaving more prudent and cautious survivors to breed. The puppeteers had also used Birthright Lotteries to try to breed humans for luck: all of Teela's ancestors for six generations are lottery winners. Speaker's outrage at learning the former forces Nessus to flee from the group and then follow from a safe distance.

While flying through a giant storm, Teela becomes separated from the others. When Louis and Speaker search for her, their flycycles are caught by an automated trap designed to catch speeders. They are brought to a floating police station where they meet Halrloprillalar Hotrufan (**Prill**), a female Pak Protector (race that built the ringworld). Prill was a former crew member of a ship that had brought back goods from worlds abandoned by the Ringworld builders. When her ship returned to the Ringworld the last time, they discovered that civilization had collapsed. Since then, Prill was living in an abandoned police station and capturing flying cars hoping to find civilized pak.

Louis surmises that a mold inadvertently brought back by a ship like Prill's mutated and broke down the superconductors vital to the Ringworld civilization, causing its fall. Nessus, using a tasp (device that remotely stimulates the pleasure center of the brain), conditions Prill into helping and joining them.

Teela rejoins them, accompanied by her new lover, a traveling warrior named **Seeker** who protected her. Based on an insight gained from studying a Ringworld map, Louis comes up with a plan to get home. Teela chooses to remain on the Ringworld with Seeker. Louis, formerly skeptical about breeding for luck, now wonders if the entire mission was caused by Teela's luck, to unite her with her true love and help her mature.

The party collects one end of the shadow-square wire that snapped after the collision with their ship and fell near their path, and drag it behind them. Louis threads it through the Lying Bastard to tether it to the floating police station. "Fist-of-God", the enormous mountain near their crash site, was not on the Ringworld map, leading Louis to guess that it is the result of a meteoroid striking the underside of the ring, pushing the ring's floor up and finally breaking through. The top of the mountain, above the atmosphere, is therefore just a hole. Louis uses the police station to drag the Lying Bastard up and into the hole. Once the ship falls through and clears the ring, they can use its hyperdrive to get home.

Louis and Speaker discuss returning to the Ringworld.

## The Ringworld Engineers

The **Hindmost**, recently deposed leader of the Puppeteers, abducts the human **Louis Wu** (who has become a wirehead) and the Kzin **Chmeee** (previously known as Speaker). Both had been part of the original Ringworld expedition. The Hindmost hopes to acquire Ringworld technology, specifically matter transmutation, to help him regain his position.

Once they reach the Ringworld, Louis and Chmeee are sent out to explore, while the Hindmost remains safely behind on their starship. Louis and Chmeee secretly plot to try to overthrow the Hindmost so they can go home. In their travels, they meet a number of the hominid species that have evolved on the Ringworld. They also learn more about the replicas (full-scale **maps**) of various Known Space worlds, including Earth, Mars and Kzin.

They discover that the Ringworld has become unstable and will collide with its star soon. The Ringworld's builders, revealed to be **Pak Protectors**, have long since died out, and the attitude jets they installed all around the rim to maintain the Ringworld's position were dismounted to use as starship engines. Chmeee goes to the Kzin map for his own goals, while Louis tries to find some way to save the trillions of inhabitants.

It is on the Map of Mars that the reunited party (and two natives) finds the Ringworld **control room** Louis is seeking, located in a vast maze of rooms in the hollow space under the map. To create the rarefied atmosphere on Mars, the Map of Mars was built 20 miles (32 km) above the main surface, creating a 4.700.000.000 km<sup>3</sup> cavity. The control room contains living space and, among other things, the meteor defense system. The defense system uses the superconductor grid embedded in the Ringworld's scrith floor to manipulate the magnetic field of the Ringworld's sun to trigger stellar flares that power a titanic gas laser (the first expedition to the Ringworld crashed after their ship was hit by this laser).

They encounter **Teela Brown**, a human member of the first expedition who had chosen to remain on Ringworld. She and her lover **Seeker** had, in the course of their travels, stumbled upon "Tree-of-Life" plants. The smell of the plant drove them to eat its roots; Teela was turned into a Pak Protector, with superhuman intelligence and strength, but Seeker died, being too old to undergo the transformation. As a Pak Protector, Teela has little control of her actions; her protective instincts force her to try to save all of the Ringworld inhabitants. This causes a dilemma. She knows of a way to save the Ringworld, but it would entail killing 5% of the people. This she cannot do. She manages to lure Louis and the others to where they can save the other 95%. Her instincts make her fight them, but she does so half-heartedly so that they can kill her.

Afterward, Louis figures out what to do. Teela had restored starship engines to their original purpose as attitude jets, but only had enough for 5% of the ring. Louis explains that the meteor defense system can generate a massive stellar flare (normally used to power the laser weapon) to provide twenty times more fuel to the attitude jets to move the Ringworld back into position. However, the radiation from the flare will be fatal to everything and everyone living on that section of the Ringworld. The actual task is carried out by the Hindmost, who is far superior at operating the control systems (and can safely pass through a Tree-of-Life plantation en route to the control center, as Louis cannot). Louis forced him to cooperate by destroying the hyperdrive.

Without hyperdrive, the party is stranded. Louis and the rest of his party look for some place to settle down, while the Hindmost remains aboard the disabled (but very safe) starship to think things over.

## The Ringworld Throne

Following the first expedition by Louis and Chmeee, the ARM (Amalgamated Regional Militia, from Earth) and the Kzin Patriarky, both major interstellar powers, send ships to the Ringworld system seeking to exploit the advanced technology and knowledge of the Ringworld's construction. They start occuping the peripheral system around the Ringworld.

A variety of Ringworld hominid species (**Ghouls**, or Night People), led by the Machine woman **Valavirgillin** (from The Ringworld Engineers), join together to kill a large nest of **vampires** (the shadow nest) which has been feeding on all of them. With the help of two Ghouls, who know that the nest is located under an abandoned floating factory, they manage to cast out the vampires. The Ghouls find one of the Hindmost's spying devices in the factory and transport it all the way to the rim, to ask for help against the Protectors who rule the rim.

Meanwhile, **Louis Wu** decides to die of old age instead of asking the Hindmost for rejuvenation, as self-punishment for helping the Hindmost to restabilize the Ringworld, killing (as he thinks) a trillion hominids to save the rest. After ten years of wandering around the Ringworld and aging, the Hindmost convinces him that he killed far fewer people than expected. Since there are signs of several Protectors on the Ringworld, Louis returns to the Hindmost to be restored to health in exchange for service. However, Louis, Hindmost, and **Acolyte** (Kzin son of Chmeee) are enslaved by **Bram**, a vampire Protector.

Bram shoots down ARM and Patriarchy ships attacking the Ringworld, and then tries to overthrow the other vampire Protectors who control the rim wall. He manages to kill them all, but returns wounded to the control center, where he fights and is killed by a Ghoul protector Louis has created. This protector is **Tunesmith**, a Ghoul transformed into a Pak Protector after Louis exposed it to the Tree-Of-Life.

## Ringworld's Children

All the intelligent species of Known Space are interested in the Ringworld. They engage in a sort of Cold War (**Fringe War**), a tense standoff involving major factions from Known Space, including the ARM and the Kzin, all vying for control of the Ringworld's secrets. The conflict is particularly dangerous due to the presence of antimatter weapons, which could cause catastrophic damage to the Ringworld itself.

Louis wakes from a prolonged healing process in a nanotech autodoc, having been severely injured in his battle against Bram, a vampire protector.The repair process has rejuvenated him to a youthful state, but he finds himself under the control of **Tunesmith**, a newly created protector from the Ghouls, who now holds significant influence over his life. Tunesmith, having absorbed vast amounts of knowledge from the Puppeteer Hindmost and other sources, is focused on managing the aftermath of Bram's reign and the escalating Fringe War.

Louis seeks a way to escape both the protector's control and the looming threat of war. He navigates a complex web of alliances and hidden agendas, including interactions with the Hindmost, who is now vulnerable and exposed after his command deck was stripped of defenses.

The conflict is resolved through the coordinated efforts of the protectors, including Tunesmith and **Proserpina** (Pak Protector and one of the original Ringworld's builders), who devise a plan to stabilize the Ringworld and neutralize the external threats.

Louis avoids being turned into a protector and escapes secretly with Hindmost back to Known Space via hyperspace, ensuring that no faction knows their destination. Hindmost returns to the Fleet of Worlds, where he gets a fragile leadership position thanks to the Ringworld data and advanced tech, and his manipulative skills. Louis and his crew end up far from Puppeteer space. 

The Ringworld's future is secured under the guardianship of its evolved inhabitants (**Children**): the descendants of the original Pak Protector species, and the vast population of hominids and other species resulting from ancient breeding experiments.

## Fate of Worlds: Return from the Ringworld

Human investigators on New Terra uncover long-buried evidence of Puppeteer wrongdoing (manipulations, abductions, and biological meddling). The mood shifts from suspicion to certainty: the Puppeteers have committed massive crimes and can no longer hide. Human leaders privately weigh the costs of retaliation against the Puppeteers. Some argue for diplomacy, others for preemptive action. All agree that Puppeteer secrecy must end.

The Hindmost receives hints that human knowledge has reached a critical threshold. He evaluates possible outcomes and begins sketching desperate contingency plans, fully aware that exposure could collapse Puppeteer civilization.

Louis and his companions realize that the galactic environment is far more hostile and politically intricate than when they departed to the Ringworld. Louis receives fragments of intelligence about Puppeteer crimes and the spiraling political crisis. He immediately recognizes that the Hindmost has kept him in the dark for decades.

Achilles, believed dead or thoroughly contained, emerges with new capabilities (advanced nanotechnology) and allies. Unlike normal Pupeteers, he feels no fear, which makes him uniquely lethal. He threatens both the Fleet and humans in a more direct, personal manner than ever before.

Rumors about threats from humans and from Achilles spread within the Fleet, pushing political factions toward extreme measures. The Hindmost mobilizes surveillance and covert agents to track Achilles. He believes Achilles’s actions could provoke humanity into annihilating the Fleet.

The Gw’oth, with their rapidly growing intellects, notice disturbances in Puppeteer space. They assign composite minds to investigate, driven partly by curiosity, partly by strategic opportunity.

Louis links up with individuals (human and nonhuman) who have reasons to distrust the Puppeteers. A loose coalition forms around the need for transparency and self-preservation.

Achilles strikes at a vulnerable Puppeteer installation, demonstrating both power and intent. He aims to seize or destroy the existing Puppeteer order. Meanwhile, New Terra dispatches scouts and intelligence vessels toward the Fleet of Worlds to gather proof strong enough to justify decisive action. News of Achilles’s resurgence and human hostility undermine the Hindmost’s authority. Political rivals question his leadership and demand explanations he cannot safely provide.

Louis and a Gw’oth envoy compare information about Puppeteer behavior, Pak activity, and potential crises. The Gw’oth express interest in the Ringworld technologies Louis brings.

Achilles manipulates Puppeteer traffic routes and communication nodes, isolating parts of the Fleet. He weaponizes fear by making it appear the humans are already attacking.

A human reconnaissance team narrowly escapes automated Puppeteer defenses. This incident convinces human leadership that negotiations will require overwhelming leverage.

Recognizing Louis’s unique perspective and experience with alien megastructures, the Hindmost reaches out, offering carefully curated “truths.” Louis doubts every word.

The Gw’oth perform simulations predicting catastrophic outcomes if Puppeteer society collapses. They conclude that intervening subtly may be in their strategic interest.

Achilles recruits disaffected Puppeteers and seizes key Fleet assets, positioning himself as the only leader capable of “saving” Puppeteer civilization.

Louis refuses the Hindmost’s half-truths and chooses his own course of action, forming alliances independent of Puppeteer oversight.

Multiple factions (Louis’s group, Hindmost’s operatives, human intelligence, and Gw’oth) converge on the Fleet of Worlds, all chasing different goals that collide.

Achilles attempts to seize control of the entire Fleet navigation network to redirect it against perceived enemies, including humans.

Louis infiltrates restricted Puppeteer zones, uncovering evidence crucial to stopping Achilles.

New Terra agents confront the Hindmost with incontrovertible proof of Puppeteer crimes. The Hindmost stalls for time, trying to avert annihilation by diplomacy and deceit.

The Gw’oth deploy a composite mind that disrupts Achilles’s communications and strategic models. Its presence terrifies Puppeteers, who see it as an unfathomable power. Achilles’s forces fragment under pressure from human operatives, Gw’oth interference, and internal Puppeteer panic. Achilles makes a last bid for escape or destruction. A climactic pursuit and battle unfolds in deep space inside the Fleet volume. Multiple parties collide; hidden technologies and last-ditch measures decide the outcome.

The truth about the Hindmost’s centuries of manipulations surfaces, causing his downfall, and murder. A new political order rises in the Fleet. Puppeteer society must adapt to a world where secrecy has failed and fear no longer guarantees safety.

New Terra secures its autonomy, becoming a fully independent and self-determining human civilizacion, no longer under Puppeteer influence or control. Humans gain knowledge that alters their standing in Known Space.

The Gw’oth accelerate into a new evolutionary phase. Individual minds merge into composite intellects, scientific innovation accelerates exponentially, and their perception of time and consequence shifts beyond human comprehension. They are on the verge of becoming a civilization that can rival the Outsiders themselves.

Louis chooses true autonomy, refusing all manipulation and embracing freedom. He leaves with more agency and clarity than ever before.
