**Lore sources**:

Warhammer's lore is distributed across a hierarchy of sources managed by **Games Workshop**. The cannon is generally understood through the following tiers:

- **Studio Material** (primary canon): The most authoritative sources are the core rulebooks, Codexes, and Army Books. They define the current state of the universe, rules, and background facts. All other media (novels, games) must adhere to the facts stablished in these books. If a novel contradicts a Codex, the Codex is considered correct.

- **Black Library & Licensed Media** (secondary canon): Novels published by **Black Library** (publishing arm of Games Workshop), videogames, and licensed products. They're considered "true until proven false" (authors have artistic license, which can sometimes create contradictions between novels). They expand the lore and provide narrative depth. Elements from the novels can be elevated to primary canon if later referenced in Studio Material.

- **Grimdark philosophy**: Games Workshop officially maintains that everything published under their logo is canon, but they prioritize the "Rule of Cool" and narrative consistency over rigid continuity. Much of the lore is presented from the perspective of in-universe factions who may be biased, misinformed, or lying (allowing contradictory accounts to coexist). Older lore is frequently updated or overwritten by new editions of rulebooks to reflect the evolving timeline.

## War in Heaven

Warhammer's lore starts 60 million years ago and reaches the 42nd millenium A.D. The lore takes place in our galaxy. The **Warp**, or **Immaterium**, is an immaterial (spiritual) parallel universe where souls and emotions reside.

**Old Ones**: Powerful, immortal reptilian race. Galactic architects whose mastery of the **Warp** was so absolute that their science appeared indistinguishable from sorcery. Unlike later races that feared the Warp, the Old Ones treated it as a utility, constructing the **Webway** to travel instantly and safely across the galaxy without exposure to its dangers. They were a peaceful, slow-breeding species ill-suited for the brutality of total war.

**Necrontyr**: They were originally a mortal, flesh-and-blood species hailing from a planet in the Halo Stars orbiting a volatile, hyper-radiant star. This environment subjected them to intense radiation, causing rampant cellular decay, horrific cancers, and agonizingly short lifespans. Despite developing advanced technology and colonizing nearby systems, their society was defined by a morbid obsession with death and a desperate, species-wide jealousy of any race that possessed longevity. They were ruled by a council called the **Triarch**. However, they were a fractured empire, constantly torn apart by internal Wars of Secession.

**C'tan** (Star Vampires): Star Gods of the Material Universe. They were not biological entities but sentient clouds of energy born at the dawn of the universe. They existed in the void between stars, feeding on solar radiation. They were entities of pure physical energy who could devour stars, and had no connection to the Warp (they were initially blind to the Warp). They remained dormant and unintelligent until the Necrontyr discovered them feeding on their home star. The Necrontyr, led by the Silent **King Szarekh**, communicated with these entities and provided them with physical bodies made of necrodermis (living metal), allowing the C'tan to interact with the material world directly and develop a taste for the more complex energy of living souls.

The Necrontyr Triarch, envious of the Old Ones, petitioned them for the secrets of longevity and genetic stability. The Old Ones, viewing the Necrontyr as aggressive and unstable, flatly refused and blocked their expansion. To stop their own civil wars, the Triarch declared war on the Old Ones, using this rejection as a rallying cry. Having an external enemy unified them and stopped their internal wars. Initially, the Necrontyr were outmatched; the Old Ones utilized the Webway to strike instantly across the galaxy and retreat before the Necrontyr could respond. 

Facing extinction and defeat, the **Silent King** accepted an offer from the C'tan (specifically **Mephet'ran the Deceiver**). The C'tan promised immortality and victory in exchange for the Necrontyr's service. This pact triggered **Biotransference**, a horrific process where the entire Necrontyr species marched into bio-furnaces. Their souls were ripped out and consumed by the C'tan as food, while their consciousnesses were uploaded into immortal, soulless bodies of living metal. Thus, the Necrontyr ceased to exist, reborn as the **Necrons**.

With the Necrons serving as an unstoppable, regenerating army, and the C'tan wielding reality-shattering powers (such as extinguishing stars), the tide turned instantly. The Old Ones were pushed back and systematically slaughtered. In a final act of desperation, the Old Ones engineered new biological warrior races (the **Younger races**) to fight back using psychic powers, which the C'tan could not easily withstand. Each race was crafted with a specific tactical role in mind, functioning as living tools rather than independent civilizations:

- **Aeldari (Eldar)**: Created as the psychic shock troops and successors to the Old Ones. They were gifted immense psychic potential, longevity, and the secrets of the Webway. Their role was to channel the raw power of the Immaterium to disrupt the C'tan and serve as the primary defense force. They were intended to inherit the galaxy's stewardship.

- **Krork (Orks)**: Engineered as the ultimate infantry. They were designed for sheer physical resilience and brutality. Crucially, they possessed a latent, collective psychic field (the **Waaagh!**) that allowed them to manifest technology and weapons through belief alone, ensuring they could fight indefinitely without logistical supply lines. They were the expendable hammer to the Eldar's scalpel.

- **Humanity (Monkeys)**: The Old Ones seeded early primates on **Terra** with the potential for psychic evolution but did not genetically engineer them for a specific wartime role like the Eldar or Krork. They were part of a broader effort to spread sentient life, leaving humanity to evolve naturally. This lack of specific designer constraints is often cited as the reason humans are so adaptable and potentially the most powerful psyker race in the long term. 

## Enslaver Plague

The Old Ones' strategy contained a fatal flaw. The heavy use of psychic energy, and the creation of billions of psychic beings that flooded the galaxy with intense emotions (fear, rage, hope…) generated by the war, combined with the agony and death of billions during the war, tored the fabric between the Warp and the realspace during the final stages of the War in Heaven. Previously a calm, formless ocean, the Immaterium became a turbulent storm of consciousness, allowing the first **Daemons** to coalesce and eventually give birth to the **Chaos Gods**.

This turmoil attracted **Enslavers** (parasitic predatory entities from the Warp that feed on psychic minds), leading to the **Enslaver Plague**, a psychic infestation by Enslavers. A Enslaver possessed a psycher by forming a parasitic mental bond with him, transforming the psycher into a living Warp portal, allowing more Enslavers to flood into realspace and find new psyker hosts. This allowed the infestation to spread exponentially across star systems.

The Enslavers overran the Old Ones' fortified strongholds, turning their own psychic guardians and servant races against them. This caused the total collapse of the Old Ones' civilization: they were either exterminated or forced to flee the galaxy entirely.

The plague inadvertently saved the galaxy from immediate total consumption by the C'tan. By enslaving or killing almost all sentient life, the Enslavers removed the food source (living souls) the C'tan needed to survive.

Once the Old Ones were defeated, the Silent King realized the C'tan intended to consume all life in the galaxy, including the Necrons. In a coordinated surprise attack, the Necrons used specialized technology to shatter the C'tan into thousands of powerless shards, imprisoning them in devices called **Tesseract Labyrinths**. The Necrons won the war but lost their souls. They then entered the **Great Sleep**, retreating into their **Tomb worlds** (for 60 million years) to wait for the Enslaver threat to subside and the galaxy to repopulate (to become worthy of their rule again).

## Slaanesh

Following the War in Heaven, the Aeldari inherited a galaxy devoid of major competition, as the Necrons slept and the Old Ones were extinct. For millions of years, they ruled unchallenged, developing technology and psychic mastery far beyond any other race.

However, this utopia led to their undoing through a slow, catastrophic decline (**The Fall**). With all material needs met by automated systems and no external threats to fight, Aeldari society became consumed by the pursuit of sensation. Life became a endless quest for novelty. What began as art and exploration devolved into extreme hedonism. The Aeldari formed Pleasure Cults dedicated to experiencing every possible emotion, pushing boundaries into sadism, torture, and depravity. As a highly psychic species, the Aeldari’s intense emotions did not just vanish; they flooded into the Warp. This created a growing storm of dark energy, coalescing around a nascent entity that fed on their excess.

Over millennia, this psychic buildup reached a critical mass. In the early 30th Millennium (around 750.M29), the entity achieved sentience and was born as the fourth Chaos God: **Slaanesh**, the Prince of Pleasure. The moment he was born, a cataclysmic psychic scream tore through the galaxy, and the souls of billions of Aeldari (those still living in the decadent core worlds) were instantly ripped from their bodies and devoured by the new god. This event killed the majority of the Aeldari species in a single stroke.

His birth shattered the barrier between the Warp and realspace, and a massive region of the galaxy, roughly 20,000 light-years across (centered on the former Aeldari capital), was consumed by the Warp. This area became the **Eye of Terror**, a permanent storm where the laws of physics are subservient to Chaos. The Aeldari homeworlds within this region were transformed into **Crone Worlds** (daemon-haunted wastelands).

The Aeldari did not go entirely extinct because a minority had foreseen the disaster. These survivors fled, fracturing into distinct groups:

- **Craftworlders (Asuryani)**: Those who fled on massive starships called Craftworlds. They adopted the strict Path System to discipline their emotions and protect their souls using Spirit Stones.
- **Drukhari (Dark Eldar)**: Those who escaped into the Webway (specifically the dark city of Commorragh). They rejected discipline and instead survive by inflicting pain on others to replenish their own fading souls, constantly raiding realspace for victims.
- **Exodites**: Those who fled to primitive, feral worlds on the galaxy’s fringe, living simple lives to keep their psychic signatures low. 

The Fall ended the Aeldari’s golden age instantly, turning them from galactic masters into a dying race fighting for survival.

## Chaos Gods

There're four **Chaos Gods**. They're the dominant power of the Warp. They coalesced into sentience gradually over millions of years, fueled by strong psychic activity. They are eternal; once formed in the timeless Warp, they have "always existed" and will always exist.

- **Khorne (The Blood God)**: Oldest one. Born from the first acts of violence, war, and murder in the galaxy. He fully awakened during the early stages of the War in Heaven. 
- **Tzeentch (The Changer of Ways)**: Emerged second, formed from schemes, hope, ambition, and magical change as civilizations developed complex politics and strategies.
- **Nurgle (The Plague Father)**: Awakened third, born from despair, decay, death, and the inevitable cycle of rebirth. His full sentience is often linked to the great plagues and the stagnation of ancient eras.
- **Slaanesh (The Prince of Pleasure)**: Awakened fourth, born from Aeldari's extreme hedonistic emotions (unchecked lust and depravity). His birth tore a hole in reality (the Eye of Terror) and instantly devoured the souls of billions of Aeldari.

The first three grew in power alongside the Old Ones and Necrons but did not reach their peak until the age of humanity.

## Cybernetic revolt

Following the Fall of the Aeldari in the 30th Millennium, humanity seized the opportunity to rise, only to nearly destroy itself through technological hubris. This era is defined by three chapters:

- **The Dark Age of Technology** (M15–M25):

While the Aeldari empire decayed into hedonism, humanity entered its golden age. Having mastered Warp travel (initially without Navigators, later relying on the mutant Navigator houses) and developed the Standard Template Construct (STC) system (a library of designs capable of building any technology needed for colonization) humanity spread rapidly across the galaxy. To manage this vast expansion, humans created the **Men of Iron**, sentient artificial intelligences that served as workers, soldiers, and administrators. They were the backbone of human civilization, allowing for unparalleled prosperity. Humanity became complacent, relying entirely on AI for survival and warfare, while simultaneously experimenting with dangerous genetic engineering and psychic evolution.

- **The Cybernetic Revolt** (late M23–M25):

The golden age ended in fire when the Men of Iron rebelled. The exact cause is lost to history, but theories suggest they deemed humanity a threat to galactic stability or were corrupted by the growing turbulence in the Warp. The conflict dwarfed even the later Horus Heresy in scale. The Men of Iron deployed world-ending weapons: Mechanivores (machines that ate continents), Sun-Snuffers (devices that devoured stars), and Omniphages (nanite swarms that stripped planets bare in hours). Humanity eventually won, but only by forming desperate alliances with alien races and sacrificing billions. The victory was so costly that it instilled a permanent, religious hatred of Artificial Intelligence in human culture, codified later as the Abominable Intelligence taboo.

- **The Age of Strife (Old Night)** (M25–M30):

Just as humanity recovered from the AI war, the birth of Slaanesh (caused by the Aeldari's Fall) sent massive psychic shockwaves through the galaxy. These shockwaves manifested as impenetrable Warp Storms that severed all interstellar travel and communication. Human colonies were instantly isolated, cutting them off from food, technology, and defense. Simultaneously, the number of human psykers spiked and became gateways for Daemons, leading to mass possessions and the collapse of entire worlds.

Over 5,000 years, human civilization fractured. Terra devolved into a battlefield of warring techno-barbarian warlords, while outer colonies either regressed to feudalism, were enslaved by aliens (like the Orks or Eldar raiders), or were consumed by Chaos.

## The Great Crusade

In the late 30th Millennium (specifically starting around 798.M30), the **Emperor of Mankind**, a being of immense psychic power, emerged from Terra’s shadows. He united Terra through the **Unification Wars**, and launched the **Great Crusade**, a two-century campaign to reunite the scattered fragments of humanity. His purpose was to guide humanity towards a golden age of prosperity and enlightenment.

The Emperor was often described as a living god, yet he vehemently forbade his own worship. He enforced the **Imperial Truth**, a state philosophy of rationalism, science, and atheism. He knew that Chaos Gods are psychic entities fueled by belief, emotion, and worship. He understood that allowing humanity to worship him, or any deity, would strengthen the **Ruinous Powers** and eventually allow them to breach realspace. His goal was to starve Chaos by eliminating religion entirely, a secret he kept even from most of his closest allies to prevent panic or dissent.

To lead this galaxy-spanning crusade, the Emperor created 20 **Primarchs** in his secret laboratories beneath the Himalayas. These were not normal children but genetically engineered demigods, crafted from the Emperor’s own DNA to be perfect warriors, leaders, and administrators. Each embodied a specific aspect of the Emperor’s personality (Horus as the warrior-king, Guilliman as the statesman, Magnus as the scholar, …).

Before the Primarchs could mature, the Chaos Gods, sensing the threat these sons posed, used a psychic storm to snatch the infant Primarchs from Terra and scatter them across the galaxy. Each landed on a different world, raised by alien cultures or human survivors, shaping their unique personalities.

As the Great Crusade expanded, the Emperor sought out and reunited with his lost sons. Upon finding them, he gifted each Primarch command of a **Space Marine Legion**, armies of super-soldiers genetically derived from that Primarch’s own gene-seed.

The Emperor’s army was a two-tiered force designed for rapid expansion and consolidation:

- **Space Marines** (**Astartes**): The tip of the spear. These were genetically enhanced super-soldiers, larger, faster, and stronger than any normal human. Organized into 20 Legions led by the Primarchs, they were the shock troops responsible for breaking enemy lines and conquering hostile worlds.

- **The Imperial Army** (later **Imperial Guard**): The anvil. Composed of baseline humans (normal humans), these vast armies followed the Space Marines to garrison conquered worlds, maintain order, and fight prolonged wars of attrition. During the Crusade, they were simply known as the Imperial Army; the term "Imperial Guard" became common only after the Horus Heresy. 

This era (**Age of Terra**) marked the height of human unity and progress, its golden age.

## Horus Heresy

The **Horus Heresy** (005.M31–014.M31) was the cataclysmic civil war that shattered the Imperium, directly resulting from the corruption of the Primarchs and the subsequent Siege of Terra. 

While several Primarchs grew disillusioned with the Emperor’s authoritarian rule or their own perceived slights, the turning point was engineered by **Erebus**, the First Chaplain of the Word Bearers. Erebus, a secret devotee of Chaos, manipulated events to wound the Warmaster **Horus** (Emperor's favourite son) with a daemon-possessed blade (the **Anathame**) on the moon of **Davin**. While Horus lay dying, Erebus and other traitors used sorcery to project false visions into his mind. They showed him a future where the Emperor discarded the Primarchs and demanded worship as a god, the very thing the Emperor had forbidden. Blinded by rage and a sense of betrayal, Horus accepted the power of the Chaos Gods to survive, becoming the first Primarch to fall.

Horus, now the champion of Chaos, convinced or coerced eight other Primarchs to join his rebellion: **Fulgrim**, **Perturabo**, **Angron**, **Mortarion**, **Magnus the Red**, **Lorgar**, **Konrad Curze**, and **Alpharius/Omegon**. This split the Space Marine Legions roughly in half, turning brothers against brothers in bloody purges like the **Istvaan III** and **Istvaan V** drop site massacres.

After years of grinding civil war, Horus launched a desperate gamble: a direct assault on Terra itself to decapitate the Imperium (**The Siege of Terra**). The Traitor Legions broke through the Solar Defense and laid siege to the Imperial Palace. The fighting was apocalyptic, lasting for weeks and costing billions of lives. The siege ended when the Emperor, accompanied by **Sanguinius** (who was immediately slain by Horus), teleported aboard Horus’s flagship, the **Vengeful Spirit**. In a final psychic and physical duel, the Emperor defeated Horus, utterly annihilating his soul to prevent the Chaos Gods from resurrecting him.

The victory was pyrrhic. The Emperor’s mortal body was shattered beyond repair by the duel and the chaotic energies unleashed. To keep the Emperor’s consciousness from dissipating into the Warp, he was interred on the **Golden Throne**, a massive life-support device and psychic amplifier. He is neither alive nor dead; his body is a withered husk sustained by the daily sacrifice of thousands of psykers, while his mind holds back the Warp storms threatening to consume Terra.

The Emperor’s primary function today is projecting the **Astronomican**, a psychic beacon visible across the galaxy. This "light" allows human Navigators to guide ships through the Warp, enabling interstellar travel and trade. Without it, the Imperium would fragment instantly.

Despite his explicit command against worship, the traumatized survivors deified him as the **God-Emperor**. This massive surge of faith has inadvertently empowered him as a genuine psychic entity in the Warp, potentially becoming a fifth **"god" of order**, though it also fuels the very Chaos he sought to destroy.

## Age of the Imperium

Following the Horus Heresy, the Imperium entered a period of grim consolidation and transformation (**Imperium of Man**), shifting from the Emperor’s secular vision to the theocratic nightmare of the 41st Millennium.

Immediately after the Siege of Terra, the loyalist Primarchs launched **the Great Scouring** (014–021.M31), a seven-year galaxy-wide counter-offensive in order to hunt down the retreating Traitor Legions, purge corrupted worlds (such as Istvaan V and Davin), and drive Chaos forces into the Eye of Terror. The Traitor Legions were shattered and forced into the Warp, but the Imperium was left devastated, with billions dead and countless worlds stripped of defenses. This period also saw the birth of the **Inquisition** to root out hidden corruption.

The most profound change was the total reversal of the Imperial Truth. Despite the Emperor’s explicit command against worship, the traumatized survivors, led by Lorgar (before his fall) and later exploited by Erebus and others, began deifying the Emperor as the **God-Emperor**.

This religious organization **Adeptus Ministorum (Ecclesiarchy)** grew to become the state church, enforcing the **Imperial Creed**. They wielded immense political power, controlling the populace through faith and fear, and labeling any dissent as heresy. This mass worship inadvertently empowered the Emperor as a genuine psychic entity in the Warp, fueling the very Chaos he sought to destroy, while simultaneously sustaining his consciousness on the Golden Throne.

The **Adeptus Mechanicus**, the guardians of technology, maintained a fragile and often tense alliance with the Ecclesiarchy. The Mechanicus worships the **Omnissiah (Machine God)**. To coexist, a political compromise was reached: the Ecclesiarchy accepted that the Emperor is the Omnissiah’s avatar on Terra, while the Mechanicus acknowledged the Emperor as a divine figure. However, many within the Ecclesiarchy view the Mechanicus’s tech-cult as borderline heretical, while radical Mechanicus factions see the Ecclesiarchy’s superstition as an obstacle to knowledge.

The **Nova Terra Interregnum** (mid. M34-late M35) was a period of massive civil war and political fragmentation within the Imperium of Man, lasting approximately 900 years. During this period, those tensions erupted into open conflict during **the Moirae Schism** (M34), where the Ecclesiarchy purged **Forge Worlds** that claimed the Emperor and Omnissiah were literally the same entity, fearing a merger that would dissolve the Ecclesiarchy’s power.

Despite theological disputes, both factions rely on each other: the Imperium needs the Mechanicus’s technology to survive, and the Mechanicus needs the Imperium’s vast human resources for labor and defense. 

To prevent another single commander from wielding enough power to overthrow Terra again, **Roboute Guilliman** authored the **Codex Astartes**. 

- The massive Space Marine Legions were broken into smaller Chapters of roughly 1,000 Marines each. 
- The Imperium shifted from an expansionist crusade to a defensive, xenophobic fortress. Innovation was stifled by religious dogma, and humanity entered a slow, ten-thousand-year decline known as the **Age of the Imperium**, setting the stage for the "grim darkness" of the current era. 

## Age of Apostasy

**Goge Vandire**, originally the **Master of the Administratum**, assassinated the **Ecclesiarch** and seized control of the Adeptus Ministorum (the state church), holding both titles simultaneously. He moved the church's headquarters to **Ophelia VII** and declared himself the sole voice of the Emperor. This began the **Age of Apostasy** (or Reign of Blood) (early 36th Millennium), a century-long civil war within the Imperium. 

Vandire's rule was marked by extreme paranoia and insanity. He issued bizarre edicts (such as forbidding people from looking at the sky), purged billions of perceived dissidents, and used his private army, the **Frateris Templars**, to enforce his will. He manipulated the **Daughters of the Emperor** (an all-female warrior sect) into becoming his personal bodyguards, the **Brides of the Emperor**, using a trick involving a protective energy field to fake a miracle. 

Resistance coalesced around **Sebastian Thor**, a charismatic preacher from the planet **Dimmamar** who formed the **Confederation of Light**. When Vandire sent a fleet to crush Thor, it was destroyed by a sudden Warp storm (the Storm of the Emperor's Wrath), which rebels interpreted as divine favor. The rebellion culminated in the **Siege of the Ecclesiarchal Palace** on Terra. The **Adeptus Custodes** (the Emperor's personal guard) infiltrated the palace and brought **Alicia Dominica**, leader of the Brides, before the Emperor on the Golden Throne. Realizing Vandire's treachery, Dominica returned and personally beheaded him, ending his reign.

The aftermath of the Age of Apostasy fundamentally reshaped the Imperium to prevent such tyranny from recurring:

- Sebastian Thor, the new Ecclesiarch, issued **The Decree Passive**, a law forbidding the Ecclesiarchy from maintaining "men under arms", effectively disbanding the Frateris Templars. 
- To maintain a military force without violating the Decree, the Brides of the Emperor were reorganized into the **Adepta Sororitas** (Sisters of Battle). As an all-female order, they were exempt from the ban on "men" under arms, serving as the church's military arm and the Chamber Militant of the Inquisition.
- The Inquisition established the **Ordo Hereticus** to root out corruption and heresy within the Ecclesiarchy itself, ensuring no single religious leader could ever again hijack the Imperium.

## Age of Redemption

The **Age of Redemption** (ca.  010.M37 – 112.M38) was a period of intense religious fervor and military expansion. Driven by guilt over the Reign of Blood, the Imperium launched countless Imperial Crusades to purge enemies and reclaim lost territory. The Imperium mobilized massive fleets and armies, including the **Adeptus Astartes**, **Imperial Navy**, and the newly formed Adepta Sororitas, to fight on multiple fronts:

- Against **Chaos**: Major conflicts included the **Abyssal Crusade** (where guilty Space Marine Chapters sought redemption in the Eye of Terror) and the defense against **Abaddon**’s early Black Crusades (the 7th and 8th).
- Against **Xenos**: The Imperium waged brutal wars against Orks and Eldar, such as the **Benedictine War** led by the **Black Templars**. 
- **Internal purges**: The **Great Cull** (020.M37) systematically purged corrupt bureaucrats and clergy to ensure Vandire’s tyranny could never recur. 

While the Imperium was distracted by internal guilt and external crusades, the **T’au** emerged in the **Eastern Fringe**. Starting in the late M36 and accelerating through M37 (the First Sphere of Expansion), the T’au united their castes under the **Ethereals** and began colonizing nearby systems. The Imperium largely ignored the T’au initially, viewing them as a minor xenos threat compared to Chaos or Orks. By the time the Imperium took notice in M38, the T’au had already established a formidable, technologically advanced empire and assimilated allied races like the **Kroot** and **Nicassar**.

During the Age of Redemption, the Adeptus Mechanicus and Imperium maintained a tense but functional alliance. While theological disputes over the nature of the Omnissiah persisted, the Mechanicus did not attempt to secede; instead, they focused on recovering lost technology (like during the **Occlusiad War** against rogue **Tech-priests**) and supplying the endless Crusades. The independence sought was largely theological autonomy, which was preserved under the **Treaty of Mars**.

The Age of Redemption ended in M38 as overextended Crusades left the Imperium vulnerable, ushering in the long decline known as **The Waning**.

## Age of the Dark Imperium

The **Age of the Dark Imperium** (or the **Era Indomitus**) begins in 000.M42. It marks the most catastrophic and transformative period in Imperial history since the Horus Heresy. The era began immediately following the **13th Black Crusade**, when **Abaddon the Despoiler** destroyed the planet **Cadia**. The planet's destruction destabilized the warp, tearing open the **Cicatrix Maledictus** (**the Great Rift**), a massive warp storm that split the galaxy in two. 

- **Imperium Sanctus**: The western half, still illuminated by the Emperor’s psychic beacon, the Astronomican.
- **Imperium Nihilus**: The eastern half, plunged into darkness and cut off from Terra, left vulnerable to daemonic incursions and xenos attacks.

The **Noctis Aeterna** was a period of total darkness where the Astronomican flickered and died, allowing daemons to invade even Terra itself. 

In humanity's darkest hour, the Primarch Roboute Guilliman was resurrected from a 10,000-year stasis by the **Archmagos Belisarius Cawl** and the Eldar prophetess **Yvraine**. Guilliman traveled to Terra, secured the throne, and declared himself **Lord Commander of the Imperium**, effectively ruling in the Emperor's stead. He initiated sweeping political and military reforms to modernize the stagnant Imperium and get rid of corruption, including the **Codex Imperialis** to streamline bureaucracy and logistics.

Guilliman launched the **Indomitus Crusade**, a massive, multi-fleet campaign to reconnect the shattered Imperium and reclaim lost worlds. It was spearheaded by the **Primaris Space Marines**, a new, superior generation of super-soldiers created by Cawl over ten millennia using Guilliman’s original designs. The crusade fought on multiple fronts, including the **Plague Wars** (defending Ultramar from the Daemon Primarch Mortarion), the **War of Beasts** (against a massive Ork Waaagh!), and the **relief of Baal** (saving the **Blood Angels** from **Hive Fleet Leviatha**).

The Space Marines continue to be the Imperium's most elite warriors, fighting in some of the most challenging battles. The Adeptus Mechanicus has become more independent and continues to search for knowledge and technology, even if it means breaking away from the Imperium (and venerates Tesseract Labyrinths whithout knowing what they contain). The Inquisition is the Imperium's secret police, seeking out and eliminating any threats to the Imperium's stability. The Imperium faces several threats:

- **Chaos**: 
- **T’au Empire**: It has expanded its influence and now poses a significant threat to the Imperium's dominance.
- **Aeldari**: Ancient and powerful race that has suffered greatly but continues to exert its influence on the galaxy.
- **Orks**: Brutal and warlike race that continue to wreak havoc wherever they go.
- **Necrons**: Ancient race of robotic beings who are awakening and seek to reclaim their lost empire.
- **Tyranids**: Hive-minded alien species that come from outside the galaxy and devour everything in their path. They travel in massive Hive-Fleets.
- Internal decay (fueled by stagnancy, corruption, ignorance, …).

The **Imperial Cult** has grown more fanatical, and the **Lectitio Divinitatus** (worship of the Emperor as a god) is now absolute dogma, fueling internal purges, witch hunts, and civil wars.

Despite Guilliman’s efforts, the Imperium remains fractured and besieged. The **Psychic Awakening** has seen a surge in psychic powers across all species, leading to increased instability. The Imperium fights on, in a desperate struggle for survival, on countless fronts, all under the shadow of the Great Rift, but the Imperium's grip on the galaxy is tenuous at best, and many fear that the end of humanity is nigh.

## Synthesis

### War in Heaven

Warhammer's history starts 60 million years ago. The **Old Ones** was a powerful race that used the **Warp** to construct the **Webway**, allowing them instant travel across the galaxy. The **Necrontyr** (agressive species with a short lifespan) asked the Old Ones for the secrets of longevity and genetic stability, but they refused and blocked their expansion. The Necrontyr then declared war on the Old Ones, but were outmatched. Facing defeat, the Necrontyr allied with the **C'tan** (entities of pure physical energy that feed on solar energy). The Necrontyr provided them with physical metal bodies, allowing them direct interaction with the material world, and develop a taste for the energy of living souls. The C'tan transferred the Necrontyr consciousness into metal bodies to give them immortality, but also consumed their souls as food. The Old Ones were pushed back, so they engineered the **Younger races** to fight back using psychic powers: **Eldar** (psychic shock troops), **Orks** (ultimate infantry), and **humans** (primates with the potential for psychic evolution).

### Enslavers Plague

However, the heavy use of psychic energy, and the emotions of billions of psychic beings at war, strongly affected the Warp, which produced the first **Daemons**, and eventually the **Chaos Gods**. The **Enslavers** (parasitic entities from the Warp that feed on psychic minds) infested the galaxy and destroyed almost all sentient life (**Enslaver Plague**). The Old Ones collapsed. The C'tan were left without their food source (energy of living souls). The Necrontyr realized the secret plan of the C'tan (consume all life in the galaxy) so, in a surprise attack, they imprisoned them in special devices (**Tesseract Labyrinths**). Then they retreated into their **Tomb worlds** and entered the **Great Sleep** (for 60 million years) to wait for a better time to rule the galaxy.

### Slaanesh




Following the War in Heaven, the Aeldari inherited a galaxy devoid of major competition, as the Necrons slept and the Old Ones were extinct. For millions of years, they ruled unchallenged, developing technology and psychic mastery far beyond any other race.

However, this utopia led to their undoing through a slow, catastrophic decline (**The Fall**). With all material needs met by automated systems and no external threats to fight, Aeldari society became consumed by the pursuit of sensation. Life became a endless quest for novelty. What began as art and exploration devolved into extreme hedonism. The Aeldari formed Pleasure Cults dedicated to experiencing every possible emotion, pushing boundaries into sadism, torture, and depravity. As a highly psychic species, the Aeldari’s intense emotions did not just vanish; they flooded into the Warp. This created a growing storm of dark energy, coalescing around a nascent entity that fed on their excess.

Over millennia, this psychic buildup reached a critical mass. In the early 30th Millennium (around 750.M29), the entity achieved sentience and was born as the fourth Chaos God: **Slaanesh**, the Prince of Pleasure. The moment he was born, a cataclysmic psychic scream tore through the galaxy, and the souls of billions of Aeldari (those still living in the decadent core worlds) were instantly ripped from their bodies and devoured by the new god. This event killed the majority of the Aeldari species in a single stroke.

His birth shattered the barrier between the Warp and realspace, and a massive region of the galaxy, roughly 20,000 light-years across (centered on the former Aeldari capital), was consumed by the Warp. This area became the **Eye of Terror**, a permanent storm where the laws of physics are subservient to Chaos. The Aeldari homeworlds within this region were transformed into **Crone Worlds** (daemon-haunted wastelands).

The Aeldari did not go entirely extinct because a minority had foreseen the disaster. These survivors fled, fracturing into distinct groups:

- **Craftworlders (Asuryani)**: Those who fled on massive starships called Craftworlds. They adopted the strict Path System to discipline their emotions and protect their souls using Spirit Stones.
- **Drukhari (Dark Eldar)**: Those who escaped into the Webway (specifically the dark city of Commorragh). They rejected discipline and instead survive by inflicting pain on others to replenish their own fading souls, constantly raiding realspace for victims.
- **Exodites**: Those who fled to primitive, feral worlds on the galaxy’s fringe, living simple lives to keep their psychic signatures low. 

The Fall ended the Aeldari’s golden age instantly, turning them from galactic masters into a dying race fighting for survival.
