## Early history

- 32,000 BC: Settlement by modern humans (Gravettian culture) in Ukraine and its vicinity dates back to this date.
- 4,500 BC: The neolithic Cucuteni-Trypillia culture flourished in wide areas of modern Ukraine (like Trypillia and the entire Dnieper-Dniester region).
- Ukraine is a probable location for the first domestication of the horse.
- Kurgan hypothesis: Volga-Dnieper region of Ukraine and southern Russia is the linguistic homeland of the Proto-Indo-Europeans (Eurasian ethnolinguistic group).
- 3rd millennium BC: Yamnaya Steppe pastoralist ancestry and Indo-European languages are spread across large parts of Europe.
- Iron age: Ukraine was inhabited by Iranian-speaking Cimmerians, Scythians, and Sarmatians.
- 700 BC-200 BC: It's part of the Scythian kingdom.
- From 6th century BC: Greek, Roman, and Byzantine colonies were established on the north-eastern shore of the Black Sea (Tyras, Olbia, Chersonesus…). They thrived into the 6th century AD.
- The Goths stayed in the area, but came under the sway of the Huns from the 370s.
- 5th-6th centuries: The Antes (a type of early Slavic people), lived in Ukraine. Migrations from Ukraine throughout the Balkans established many South Slavic nations. Northern migrations, almost reaching Lake Ilmen, produced the Ilmen Slavs and the Krivichs.
- 482 AD: Kyiv was founded (the exact year is still debated).
- 602 AD: An Avar raid in 602 collapsed the Antes Union, but most Antes survived as separate tribes until the beginning of the 2nd millenium. 
- 7th century: Eastern Ukraine was the centre of Old Great Bulgaria. At the end of the century, most Bulgar tribes migrated and the Khazars took over much of the land.


## Kievan Rus state

- 8th-9th centuries: Varangians from Scandinavia (vikings) settle in Belarus, Russia, and Ukraine.
- 862 AD: A group of Varangians known as the Rus, leaded by Rurik, settle in Novgorod. 
- 882 AD: The Rus, leaded by prince Oleg, conquer Kiev from other Varangians, and proclaims it as the new capital of the Rus.
- 10th-11th centuries: Golden age. Kievan Rus became the largest and most powerful state in Europe.
- Vladimir the Great (980-1015) introduces Christianity.
- 12th century: Kievan Rus disintegrates into separate principalities.
- 11th-12th centuries: The nomadic onfederacy of the Turkic-speaking Cumans and Kipchaks dominate the Pontic steppe north of Black Sea.
- Mid-13th century: The Mongol invasions devastate Kievan Rus. 
- 1240 AD: Siege of Kiev. The mongols destroy the city.
- 1253 AD: After Daniel of Galicia reunites much of south-western Rus (including Kyiv, Volhynia, and Galicia), he's crowned first king of Ruthenia.

## Foreign domination

- 1340-1392: Galicia-Volhynia Wars. After the death of the king of Ruthenia (Yuri II Boleslav), Lithuania and Poland advance claims over the kingdom. Finally, the region is partitioned between Poland and Lithuania, and Ruthenia disappears.
- 1430: The region of Podolia is incorporated to Poland. Ukraine becomes increasingly settled by Poles.
- 1441: Prince Haci I Giray, a Genghis Khan descendant, founds the Crimean Khanate on the Crimean Peninsula and surrounding steppes. The next three centuries it orchestrates Tatar slave raids.
Mid-13th to late-15th century: The Republic of Genoa founds numerous colonies on the northern coast of the Black Sea, which became large commercial centres headed by a consul.
- 1569: Poland and Lithuania create a single state called the Polish-Lithanian Commonwealth (Union of Lublin). Most Ukrainian lands were transferred to Poland.
- Under Polonisation pressures, many landed gentry of Ruthenia convert to Catholicism and join the Polish nobility.


## Cossack Hetmanate

- Mid-17th century: Ruthenian peasants (looking for protection) and Dnieper Cossacks form a Cossack military quasi-state, the Zaporozhian Host (or Cossack Hetmanate).
- 1648: Bohdan Khmelnytsky led the largest Cossack uprising, widely supported by local population, against the Commonwealth.
- 1651: Battle of Berestechko. Khmelnytsky suffers a crushing defeat and turns to the Russian tsar for help. He makes an alliance with Russia and acknowledges loyalty to the tsar (Pereiaslav Agreement).
- 1657-1686: The Ruin. A devastating 30-year war for the control of the Cossack Hetmanate amongst Russia, Poland, Crimean Khanate, Ottoman Empire, and Cossacks. Finally, Russia and Poland divide the Hetmanate lands between them (Treaty of Perpetual Peace).
- 1686: The Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople (leader of the Easter Orthodox Church) allow the Moscow Patriarchate annex the Metropolitanate of Kyiv.
- 1700-1721: Great Northern War. A Russia-led coallition contested supremacy of Sweden in North, Central, and East Europe. The Cossack hetman Ivan Mazepa defected to the Swedes in a bid to get rid of Russian dependence, but Baturyn (Hetmanate's capital) was sacked (1708), and Cossacks were crushed in the Battle of Poltava (1709). After this, the Hetmanate's autonomy was severely restricted.
- 1768: The last major Cossack uprising (the Koliivshchyna) was suppressed by Russia.
- 1764-1781: Catherine the Great incorporated much of Central Ukraine into the Russian Empire, abolishing the Cossack Hetmanate and the Zaporozhian Sich.
- 1783: Russia annexes Crimea.
- Russification: Policy established by the tsar in the new lands (Novorossiya). Ukrainian language is suppressed.
- 1795: Fall of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth. Western Ukraine is split between Russia and Austria.


## 19th and early 20th century

- 19th century: Ukrainian nationalism rises (Taras Shevchenko, Mykhailo Drahomanov). It's relatively lenient in the Austrian part, but severly restricted in the Russian part.
- 1861: Serfdom is abolished in the Russian Empire by Alexander II. This allowed Ukraine join the Industrial Revolution, although later than most Western Europe.
- Ukrainian diaspora: The great poverty in the Austrian part forced hundred of thousands into emigration. Some of them settled in the Russia's Far East.
- 1914-1918: World War I. Ukranians were split between Austria-Hungary (part of the Central Powers) and Russia (part of the Triple Entente).
- 1917: Russia Empire collapse.
- 1917-1921: Russia Civil War. It caused 1.5 million deads.
- 1917-1921: Ukrainian War of Independence. Ukrainians fight alongside or against the Red (soviet), White (anti-bolshevik), Black (anarchist communism), and Green (peasants) armies, with the Poles, Hungarians, and Germans. Finally, Poland annexed Western Ukraine, and Russia established the Soviet Ukraine.
- A few Ukrainian independent states were created, but didn't last long due to unstable political and military environment. The army was regularly overrun by other forces (including Poland and Hungary), and the country also suffered a coup d'état.
- 1921: Russian famine.
- 1922: Creation of the Soviet Union.
