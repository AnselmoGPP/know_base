## DHT

**DHT** (dihydrotestosterone) hormone is an androgen more potent than testosterone. It binds more strongly to androgen receptors and has a longer half-life. It plays a key role in:

- Sexual development: Formation of male external genitalia (penis, scrotum) during fetal development.
- Puberty: Growth of facial, body, and pubic hair, deepening of the voice, and enlargement of the penis and prostate.
- Prostate health: Growth and function.
- Hair regulation: Promotes body hair growth, but contributes to scalp hair loss (androgenetic alopecia) in genetically predisposed individuals.
- Sebum production: Stimulates oil glands, influencing skin health and acne.
- Sexual behavior and libido: Works with testosterone to support male sexual drive.

## 5α-reductase

The enzyme **5α-reductase** converts **testosterone** into **DHT** (dihydrotestosterone) in specific tissues, including prostate, skin, hair follicles, liver, testes, and ovaries. This conversion occurs locally, meaning DHT acts primarily as a paracrine hormone (exerting effects in the tissues where it's made, rather than circulating widely). About 5-10% of daily testosterone is converted to DHT.

There are three types of 5α-reductase (types 1, 2, and 3). Type 2 is the most active in male development. DHT can also be synthesized via "backdoor pathways" that bypass testosterone, especially during fetal development.

## DHT dual behavior

Depending on the location and the genetic sensitivity of the hair follicles, DHT can promote:

- **Hair growth**: Beard, chest, and pubic region. Development of thick hair and prolonging the anagen phase.

- **Hair loss**: Scalp (particularly in the hairline, temples, and crown). Follicles shrink over time, and the anagen phase is shortened, producing progressively thinner, shorter, weaker hairs until they stop growing altogether (follicular miniaturization).

The degree of growth and loss depends on the genetic sensitivity of the individual to each response.

Reportedly, testosterone by itself doesn't cause hair loss, DHT does. The hair loss caused by DHT is called androgenetic alopecia.

The regional difference is not due to DHT levels, but to how each follicle's genetics determine its response

## Mechanisms

DHT binds to androgen receptors in the cells. Scalp hair follicles have higher sensitivity to DHT due to greater androgen receptor activity and gene expression.

A DHT molecule enters the cell and binds to an androgen receptor (AR) in the cytoplasm. The DHT-AR complex travels to the nucleus and binds AREs (Androgen Response Elements), specific DNA sequences that regulate gene expression.

Different hair follicles express different sets of genes due to epigenetic programming and local regulatory factors. In androgenetic alopecia, scalp follicles express genes that

- Increase TGF-β (a growth inhibitor)
- Suppress Wnt/β-catenin (a key pathway for hair growth)

Scalp follicles in balding-prone areas have higher androgen receptor denstiy and different co-regulator proteins, which change how the DNA responds to DHT.

Gene expression is region-specific.

The different response to DHT in beard vs. scalp follicles is due to differences in co-regulator proteins, gene expression programs, and local enzymatic activity

Here’s what’s happening deeper:

1. Co-regulator Proteins:
  - The AR-DHT complex recruits co-activators or co-repressors that vary by follicle type. 
  - In beard follicles, co-activators promote expression of growth genes like IGF-1, FGF, and Wnt/β-catenin pathway components.
  - In scalp follicles (balding-prone), different co-regulators drive expression of inhibitory signals like TGF-β1, DKK1, and IL-6, which suppress hair growth and promote fibrosis. 
2. Gene Regulation via Epigenetics:
  - Though DNA is identical, epigenetic marks (like DNA methylation or histone modifications) determine which genes are accessible.
  - Scalp and beard follicles have different chromatin landscapes, so the same AR-DHT complex activates distinct gene sets. 
3. Local Enzyme Activity (5α-Reductase):
  - Beard follicles express high levels of 5α-reductase type 2 (SRD5A2), efficiently converting testosterone to DHT.
  - This local amplification enhances androgen signaling specifically in facial hair. 
4. Paracrine Signaling Differences:
  - DHT-stimulated dermal papilla (DP) cells in beard follicles secrete mitogenic factors (e.g., IGF-1) that promote growth. 
  - In balding scalp DP cells, DHT induces TGF-β1 and DKK1, which inhibit Wnt signaling and trigger follicular miniaturization. 
So, while DHT binds the same receptor and enters the nucleus in both follicles, the cellular context—co-regulators, epigenetic state, and local gene networks—determines the outcome.


Here’s what actually happens:

- DHT binds to androgen receptors (AR) in genetically sensitive scalp follicles, especially in dermal papilla cells (DPCs). 
- The DHT-AR complex enters the nucleus and alters gene expression — not by causing protein buildup, but by activating specific genes like TGF-β1, DKK1, and IL-6, which inhibit hair growth and shorten the anagen (growth) phase. 
- This leads to follicular miniaturization, not protein accumulation. 
- Over time, DHT signaling can trigger apoptosis (programmed cell death) in DPCs and keratinocytes, contributing to hair loss. 

So while apoptosis is involved, it’s not due to excess proteins clogging the cell. Instead, DHT activates genetic programs that promote cell death and suppress regeneration — especially in balding-prone areas.
